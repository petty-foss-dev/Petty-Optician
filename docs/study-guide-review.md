# Making Optician a complete Basic exam study guide

Reviewed 19 November 2026 against revision `f72c644` and the July 2026 Basic candidate handbook. The audience is a first-time candidate for the ABO spectacle exam (NOCE) or the NCLE contact lens exam (CLRE). Practical and Advanced have their own specifications. Keep them identifiable as separate qualifications if the app offers those tracks later. [Official handbooks](https://www.abo-ncle.org/ABONCLE/ABONCLE/Exams/ABO-NCLE-Exam-Handbooks.aspx)

## Where a learner needs more help

Every published Basic domain has a chapter and its configured exam weight matches the official outline. The app already has ordered reading, notes, flashcards, weak-topic review, timed mocks and 11 native trainers. The biggest opportunity is the step between reading a definition and making a decision independently. [ABO specifications](https://www.abo-ncle.org/ABO/BasicCert/ABOContent_TestSpecifications.aspx) · [NCLE specifications](https://www.abo-ncle.org/ABO/BasicCert/NCLEContent_TestSpecifications.aspx)

| Observation in the shipped content | Consequence for a beginner |
| --- | --- |
| The 133 lesson bodies have a median length of 100 words. Of those, 65 are under 100 words and 14 explicitly embed a diagram. Counts exclude chapter introductions, recaps and other material reachable from the reader. | Some subjects fit on a reference card. A student learning an unfamiliar procedure also needs to see someone work through it. |
| The 681 library resources link to chapters by domain. ABO optics has 147 matches and NCLE diagnostic fitting has 129. | A student stuck on one step can reach the right shelf and still have to choose among over a hundred items. |
| All 1,193 questions have explanations. Of those, 436 have an item-level source. Thirty of 133 lessons have their own source list. Question-to-lesson links are inferred from text overlap. | A missed answer can lead to a broad chapter or an ambiguous match. A chapter bibliography alone can be hard to trace to a specific rule. |
| The exam-date plan calculates its daily target from unseen questions. Today also has a separate continue-reading card. | A new learner who studies a chapter can make progress without meeting the target the countdown emphasizes. |

Counts come from the bundled content files, the reader, exam plan and trainer registry. They measure what the app exposes, not clinical accuracy or individual learners’ readiness. This review sampled content and navigation code. It did not grade every question or check the availability of every external resource.

## First release to build

1. **Teach an ABO optics problem from beginning to end.** Two source lessons have the same heading, “Prism and Prentice’s rule.” Give them distinct purposes. In the first, show a labeled optical center and pupil and explain why displacement matters. In the second, work a complete order with units visible. For example, 5 mm through a +4.00 D spherical lens produces 2 prism diopters in magnitude. Show why 5 mm becomes 0.5 cm, ask the learner to predict base direction from a labeled drawing, then open the existing Prism & Base trainer. Finish with a different order and explain the likely millimeter-to-centimeter error. Keep each lesson’s saved identity.
2. **Teach an NCLE case across two visits.** Dispensing and follow-up each account for 20% of the CLRE. Their chapters already contain 17 lessons and 178 questions combined. At dispensing, have the fictional wearer explain their care routine back to the learner. At follow-up, reveal a symptom, then an observation. Ask the learner to distinguish what the wearer reported from what the optician observed, document each, and select the appropriate next action from a reviewed answer key. Link to the existing Soft Lens Fit or Lens Problems trainer where relevant. Include feedback on each decision, an accessible text description of each visual and sources for care and referral guidance. [NCLE specifications](https://www.abo-ncle.org/ABO/BasicCert/NCLEContent_TestSpecifications.aspx)
3. **Put the next resource inside the lesson.** For each pilot lesson, handpick one visual or short reading and one practice activity. Give each link a reason to open it, such as “Watch how the pupil and optical center are marked.” Store explicit lesson, resource, question and trainer references for these selections. Open the same lesson from the resource or a missed checkpoint. Retain the full chapter library and the existing automatic matches for wider browsing. The explanation and worked example should remain usable offline.
4. **Give Today a reading assignment.** Pair the next unread lesson with its example and a short check. A returning learner can resume at the same place or go directly to due review. Count completing a lesson toward the reading plan. Show checkpoint performance and timed mock readiness separately. A first-time learner should be able to start either track without first taking a test.

**Pilot acceptance.** On each track, a fresh install leads from Today to the first lesson. A learner can finish the pilot example offline, answer a new problem, follow a missed answer back to its explanation, and resume after quitting. A saved note on either existing prism lesson still opens on the original passage. Ask a first-time learner to explain the unit conversion or separate a symptom from an observation before expanding the pattern across all chapters.

## Editorial work after the pilot

- **Track individual objectives.** Map each handbook subobjective to the lesson that teaches it, an example and an independently answerable check. Start with NCLE instrumentation. The current OCT lesson explains clearance, while the handbook also asks about landing zones. Add an annotated cross-section contrasting the central clearance measurement with the peripheral landing area. Review rigid-lens bevel inspection against the handbook’s instrument list. Record missing coverage, alternate terminology and reviewer sign-off at the objective level. [Basic handbook, NCLE domains III and VII](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1EjE8GtE_jfLiGeA2x3zAPOroAOtF-T3a/view)
- **Prioritize source review where an error matters most.** Give standards, legal rules, clinical warning signs and care instructions a specific source, effective or review date, and a qualified reviewer. Use the current registration notice for fees. The October 2026 increase to $275 supersedes the older fee in the July handbook. Store the date next to the fact so the next editor can resolve the discrepancy. [Official fee update](https://abo-ncle.org/iCore/Events/Event_display.aspx?EventKey=NC20260701&WebsiteKey=be2595ca-e2ac-42b6-b7c7-abc277b86326)
- **Tighten mock exam reporting.** The current builder weights the full set of 125 and then randomly excludes 25 from scoring. Build the 100 scored slots to the published domain counts first, choose 25 distinct practice pilot items, then shuffle the two sets together. Test the scored breakdown for each exam. Describe the fixed split as the app’s mock format and keep the three-mock, 75% readiness benchmark labeled as an app target. [Basic handbook, test outlines and scoring](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1EjE8GtE_jfLiGeA2x3zAPOroAOtF-T3a/view)
- **Expand by learner difficulty.** After the pilots, prioritize ABO frame fitting and verification, and NCLE instrument interpretation. Reuse the existing measurement trainers, photos, cases and glossary. Choose new illustrations based on what a learner must distinguish, such as two different markings or two similar patterns. A completed chapter should let someone explain a decision and apply it to an unfamiliar case.
