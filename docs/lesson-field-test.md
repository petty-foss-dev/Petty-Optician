## Candidate walkthrough

Start from Study. Select the track you are preparing for.

Try the lesson checkpoint, then open the trainer. Tell the observer where you hesitated or whether a diagram, label or term led you to a different conclusion. If you use VoiceOver or larger text, try your usual settings.

**Fresh cases.** Close the lesson and explain your reasoning aloud or in a few sentences.

- **ABO.** A wearer measures 35 mm OD and 31 mm OS at distance. Their adjusted single-vision frames have both optical centers marked 33 mm from the bridge. Both lenses are +5.00 D spheres with no prescribed prism. Does the total separation match? For each eye, locate its optical center relative to the pupil and predict prism amount and base direction.
- **NCLE.** A settled soft trial lens looks centered and the wearer feels comfortable. It barely moves on the blink. With gentle push-up the examiner finds clear resistance. A second trial moves readily on push-up and slides excessively on the blink. Describe each observation, interpret each fit, and explain what you would still assess before accepting either trial.

Record the candidate’s first explanation before showing the discussion below. Include one phrase or visual detail that helped and one that needs revising.

## Optician or instructor review

**ABO key.** Both totals are 66 mm. OD has a 2 mm nasal optical center relative to its pupil. OS has a 2 mm temporal optical center relative to its pupil. Each lens induces 0.2 cm × 5.00 D = 1Δ. At OD the base points in, and at OS it points out. Score the total, each monocular relationship, the unit conversion and each base direction separately. Check whether the front-view drawing makes the patient’s right and left unambiguous.

**NCLE key.** Minimal blink movement together with push-up resistance supports a tight fit for the first trial. The second trial shows excessive blink movement and easy displacement, supporting a loose fit. Credit a candidate who considers comfort and centration alongside the objective signs and asks for coverage, vision, recovery or an ocular-surface assessment. The stills show simulated phases. Review whether the views convey the findings and whether the captions provide equivalent information when the image cannot be seen.

For either lesson, identify the precise term, observed sign, figure or calculation that needs correction. Give the reason and a reference if available. Distinguish the candidate’s unaided answer from any answer given after a prompt. Send de-identified comments through TestFlight.
